# LATAM impact and payment methods

**LAC** means Latin America and the Caribbean. This project uses **LATAM** for its three countries: Mexico, Colombia and Argentina.

World Bank data reports 2024 debit-card ownership of **64.1% of adults in Argentina, 40.1% in Mexico and 26.1% in Colombia**. Ownership is not transaction share, but it shows the reach of issuer-side card service. IDB research also identifies trust and understanding as barriers to digital finance.

## Regional systems in plain language

| System | Country | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| SPEI | Mexico | Banco de México's electronic interbank transfer system |
| Bre-B | Colombia | Interoperable instant-payment ecosystem led by Banco de la República |
| PCT | Argentina | `Pagos con Transferencia`, an interoperable transfer-payment model often initiated by QR |
| Card | All | Bank-issued credential routed through a network such as Visa |
| Wallet | All | Interface funded by balance, transfer or card; often a channel rather than the final rail |
| QR | All | Payment initiation method; the underlying rail may be transfer, wallet balance or card |
| ATM | All | Terminal for cash and account services; disputes include unauthorized or failed cash withdrawal |

## Earlier cross-rail prioritization

Before the team narrowed the product to Visa, payment methods were scored equally on volume and operational impact.

| Country | First priority | Second priority | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mexico | SPEI, 5.0/5 | Cards, 4.5/5 | Transfer scale and urgency; high card evidence burden |
| Colombia | Account and immediate transfers, 5.0/5 | Cards, 4.0/5 | Strong transfer use and instant-payment growth |
| Argentina | Transfers, payment accounts and PCT, 5.0/5 | Credit cards, 4.0/5 | Transfers dominated operations; credit cards were prominent in complaints |

This is background research. The current scope is Visa issuer card disputes because the team chose the call-center workflow and has a detailed Visa process reference.

## LATAM-specific challenges

- Country consumer rules coexist with Visa rules and issuer policy.
- Spanish varies by country and assisted-service dependence is uneven.
- QR and wallets can hide the rail that determines the procedure.
- Plain language, accessibility and a complete assisted path are essential to trust.
- National sources use different denominators, so raw country figures are not directly comparable.
