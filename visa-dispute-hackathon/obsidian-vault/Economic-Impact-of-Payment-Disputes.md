# Economic impact of payment disputes

The cost of a dispute is broader than the disputed amount.

| Party | Direct impact of a poor process | Business consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Cardholder | Money unavailable, continued exposure, repeated explanations and uncertain status | Complaints, attrition and reduced card use |
| Issuer | Investigation labor, provisional-credit funding, fraud loss and deadline risk | Higher cost, backlog, lost recovery and conduct risk |
| Merchant and acquirer | Revenue reversal, evidence workload, fees and monitoring | More contested cases and slower resolution |
| Payment ecosystem | More friction and less confidence in digital commerce | Reduced use and pressure for stronger controls |

## External scale indicators

- Visa reported **106 million disputes globally in 2025**, **35% more than in 2019**. This is not a LATAM estimate.
- Visa cites Auriemma Research reporting that **41% of surveyed debit-card holders who disputed a purchase were less likely to use the card afterward**.
- World Bank data shows adults making or receiving digital payments in Latin America and the Caribbean increased from **43% in 2014 to 59% in 2024**.

## Business value measures

| Value mechanism | Measures |
|---|---|
| Reduce handling | Cost per successful resolution; clerical time; handling time after quality gates |
| Protect recovery | Recovery value, missed-deadline value and net loss by condition |
| Reduce customer effort | Same-case recontact, transfers, repeat-story rate and comprehension |
| Improve control | Evidence completeness, invalid-action blocks, deadline accuracy and duplicate actions |
| Preserve trust | CSAT, escalation, appeal and false-denial guardrails |

The synthetic data cannot calculate real loss, recovery or causal savings. Production financial and network-case data are required.
