# Disputes, fraud, refunds and chargebacks

A **payment dispute** begins when a cardholder asks the issuer to investigate or reverse a transaction. The customer's statement starts an allegation and a formal process; it does not prove fraud or determine liability.

| Term | Plain-language meaning | Initiator |
|---|---|---|
| Dispute | Formal investigation of a transaction and, when allowed, a request for reversal | Cardholder through issuer |
| Fraud | Unauthorized use or deception; evidence must establish what happened | Customer, issuer or network may detect or allege it |
| Refund | Merchant voluntarily returns money | Merchant |
| Chargeback | Network-governed reversal from issuer through Visa to the acquirer | Issuer when rules permit |
| Provisional credit | Temporary credit while investigation continues | Issuer under law and policy |

## Refund versus chargeback

A refund follows the commercial relationship: the merchant agrees to return money. A chargeback follows the card-network process: the issuer uses a valid condition, evidence and deadlines to seek a reversal. A dispute can end without either outcome.

## Common card problems

| Customer language | Allegation | Possible Visa family after evidence |
|---|---|---|
| “I did not make this purchase” | Unauthorized purchase | 10.x Fraud, or 11.x if records establish an authorization failure |
| “I was charged twice” | Processing error | 12.6.1 Duplicate processing |
| “The amount or currency is wrong” | Processing error | 12.3 or 12.5 |
| “My order never arrived” | Merchant dispute | 13.1 |
| “I cancelled the subscription” | Merchant dispute | 13.2 |
| “The merchant promised a refund” | Merchant dispute | 13.6 |
| “The ATM debited me but gave no cash” | Cash-dispensation problem | 13.9 |

An unfamiliar merchant descriptor may become a recognized purchase. Transaction, authorization, entry-mode, merchant and delivery evidence can change the applicable route. The system therefore separates the **customer allegation** from the **Visa condition**.
